TermDefinition
5
Active Ingredient (AI)The chemical compound responsible for the pesticidal effect; a product may contain several in combination.
6
Mode of Action (MoA)The biochemical mechanism by which a pesticide kills or inhibits a pest; same MoA = same resistance selection.
7
Site of Action (SoA)The specific plant target location of a herbicide; HRAC's classification basis.
8
Cross-resistanceOne resistance mechanism conferring resistance to multiple pesticides; universal within an MoA group.
9
Negative cross-resistanceA resistance mutation increasing sensitivity to another pesticide (e.g., FRAC 1 <-> FRAC 10).
10
Target-site / non-target-site resistance (TSR/NTSR)Mutation at the binding protein vs. uptake/detoxification changes (e.g., P450 metabolic resistance spanning MoA groups).
11
Resistance risk ratingRAC assessment (High/Medium/Low/Unknown) of how quickly resistance emerges for a group.
12
PremixA single formulated product with two or more AIs (FRAC notation: A/B; guarantees dose of both partners).
13
Tank mixTwo separate products combined in the spray tank (notation: A+B; requires compatibility verification and jar test).
14
PHI / REIPre-harvest interval / re-entry interval - minimum days before harvest / worker re-entry.
15
Systemic / contact / translaminarTranslocated through vascular tissue / acts at contact point only / moves locally within the leaf.
16
Protectant / curative fungicidePrevents infection (M-series foundation of programmes) / stops established infection (single-site, higher risk).
17
kdrKnock-down resistance - pyrethroid target-site mutation (L1014F, T929I) in the sodium channel.
18
PTACPesticide Technical Advisory Committee - the advisory body to which this mapping is submitted.
